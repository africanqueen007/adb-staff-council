# Episode Formats: The Genre Wheel

**Every episode is a different genre. The host and the facts stay the same.**

Draft, 8 November 2026

---

## 1. The Rule: Change the Wrapper, Keep the Host

Jordan Howlett switches formats all the time, but he is always recognizably himself. On his YouTube channel (observed 8 November 2026) you can find:[^yt]

- a cooking-show mockumentary ("We don't have a name for the show yet," 8:05) and a cooking series ("Episode 2: Blueberry Compote," 14:19);
- a corporate sketch in which he keeps giving himself grander job titles ("Jordan is the CCO," 1:00);
- a travel vlog ("I Went to the Milan Cortina Winter Olympics," 5:31);
- an advice compilation in which he plays a "part time internet lawyer" giving "legal romantic advice" (8:21);
- his core "Fast Food Secrets Club" format.

He also played Commissioner Gordon in a State Farm commercial.[^upworthy] Different wrapper every time, same host.

Your version: **the genre changes every episode, and five things never change.**

### The Five Constants

1. **"Pull up a chair."** It opens every episode, in the genre's style: sung in the musical, whispered in the nature documentary, shouted in the game show.
2. **The Binder appears,** dressed for the genre: the heist blueprint, the courtroom evidence, the cookbook.
3. **The Recipe and the Receipt.** The exact steps, dates, and the real policy page appear plainly on screen in every episode, however silly the genre. This is the one thing that never goes in costume.
4. **The closing question and the sign-off:** "But what do I know. I only read the whole thing."
5. **Captions, 5–10 minutes, and one companion Short.**

### The Genre Wheel (a Recurring Bit)

A real spinning wheel in the cold open "decides" the genre. Even better: the closing question of each episode lets staff vote on the next one. *"Next time: musical or heist? Vote here."* The switching becomes something staff take part in, not just watch.

## 2. Fourteen Formats

| # | Format | One-line premise | Best for | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Musical** | You sing the policy; the chorus is the steps | Deadlines and step-by-step lists | High |
| 2 | **The Heist** | The crew plans to "recover" a benefit before it expires | Use-it-or-lose-it benefits, deadlines | Medium–high |
| 3 | **The Nature Documentary** | A whispered narrator observes staff meeting the new rule in their "natural habitat" | Changes to everyday behavior: office space, meetings, new tools | Low |
| 4 | **The Courtroom** | "The Staff vs. the Old Policy": the old rule is on trial | Explaining *why* a rule changed, with both sides heard | Medium |
| 5 | **The Cooking Show** | The recipe for a claim: documents are the ingredients | Applications that need paperwork | Low–medium |
| 6 | **The Game Show: "Eligible or Not?"** | Colleagues buzz in on made-up scenarios | Eligibility rules with tricky edge cases | Medium |
| 7 | **The Infomercial: "But Wait, There's More!"** | An over-the-top pitch for a benefit nobody uses | Underused benefits and services | Low |
| 8 | **Breaking News** | Anchor desk, a field reporter at the help desk, and a "deadline forecast" | Urgent changes and effective dates | Low–medium |
| 9 | **The Soap Opera** | Dramatic zooms and gasps: "She… never submitted the form." | What happens if you miss a step (gently) | Medium |
| 10 | **The Horror Trailer** | "This year… the deadline is coming." | Deadline reminders, especially around Halloween | Low |
| 11 | **Sports Commentary** | Two commentators narrate a colleague completing a process like a cup final | Multi-step processes | Low–medium |
| 12 | **The Picture-Book Read-Aloud** | The Chair reads an illustrated storybook version at bedtime pace | Complex or sensitive policies that need calm and clarity | Medium |
| 13 | **The Travel Vlog: "Mission Mail Road Trip"** | The Chair "visits" resident missions by video call | Policies that work differently at headquarters and in the field | Medium |
| 14 | **The Detective Story: "The Case of the Missing Form"** | Black and white, a trench coat, a gravelly voiceover | Troubleshooting: where requests get stuck and who can help | Medium |

### Notes on Each Format

**1. The Musical.** Write the chorus first: it should be the three steps or the one deadline, so the hook *is* the policy. Colleagues can be backing singers. After the song, show the Receipt card plainly. **Music must be original or properly licensed.** Do not put new words to a famous song: the melody still belongs to its songwriter. Save this one until the team has a few episodes of practice.

**2. The Heist.** The Chair assembles a crew, one specialist per step. The heist blueprint is your "Follow the Form" diagram. Slow-motion team walk down the corridor. The "vault" is the benefit; the "laser grid" is the deadline.

**3. The Nature Documentary.** The calmest and cheapest format: a hushed voiceover over footage of colleagues going about their day. *"Here, the staff member approaches the new booking system. Cautiously."* "Field notes" cards carry the facts. It suits the Calm Insider tone and Jordan's whisper.

**4. The Courtroom.** The old policy is in the dock; witnesses explain what was wrong with it; the "verdict" is what changed. This is the natural home for the Unc-style two-sided question. **Keep it clearly fictional and about a document, not people.** Do not parody ADB's real grievance, appeals, or Administrative Tribunal processes.

**5. The Cooking Show.** The ingredients are the documents you need; the method is the steps; "here's one I prepared earlier" is the completed form. This is closest to Jordan's own mockumentary, so make the gag yours: the Chair can't find the bowls, *and* can't find the form.

**6. The Game Show: "Eligible or Not?"** Colleagues buzz in on short scenarios: "Hired in March, moved missions in June. Eligible?" The reveal shows the rule on screen. **Use only invented scenarios, never real staff cases.** Do not use a real show's name, logo, set, or music.

**7. The Infomercial.** Bright colors, fake testimonials from colleagues (scripted and clearly a joke), and "But wait, there's more!" The "call now" card is how to apply. Ideal when a useful benefit simply isn't being used.

**8. Breaking News.** An anchor desk, a field reporter "live" at the help desk, and a weather segment that becomes the deadline forecast. A good fit for changes that take effect soon.

**9. The Soap Opera.** Over-the-top drama about a small mistake: a forgotten signature, a missed date. The consequence is real, but the tone is gentle and ends with exactly how to avoid it. Play the drama, not stereotypes.

**10. The Horror Trailer.** Ninety seconds of "coming soon" dread for an approaching deadline, ending with the calm fix. Keep it funny, never anxiety-inducing, and never use it for sensitive topics.

**11. Sports Commentary.** Two commentators (you and a colleague) narrate someone completing a process: tactics board, instant replays, "And she's submitted it before the deadline! What a finish!"

**12. The Picture-Book Read-Aloud.** The Chair, in a cardigan, reads a simple illustrated story version of the policy at bedtime pace. The illustrations can be made in Canva. This is the right format when the topic is heavy and needs warmth and clarity more than jokes.

**13. The Travel Vlog.** The Chair "tours" resident missions through short video calls and selfie clips from colleagues, showing how the change applies in different places. It keeps the series from being headquarters-only.

**14. The Detective Story.** A trench coat, black and white, rain on the window. The case: a request that has disappeared somewhere in the process. The solution: where it got stuck, and exactly who to contact.

## 3. A Suggested Season 1 Lineup

These pair formats with the Staff Council's six areas of work.[^sa] Swap them as the real policy changes come in.

| Episode | Area | Format | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (pilot) | All | **Mockumentary with street interviews** (as written in the pilot script) | Introduces the host and the idea before any genre games |
| 2 | Compensation and benefits | **The Infomercial** or **The Heist** | Benefits are things to "sell" or to claim before they expire |
| 3 | Talent and performance management | **Sports Commentary** or **The Game Show** | Processes with steps and rules |
| 4 | Equity and inclusion | **The Travel Vlog** | Real voices from across ADB, sincere in tone |
| 5 | Workplace culture and justice | **The Nature Documentary** for culture topics; **The Picture-Book** for justice processes | Turns the comedy down where it needs to be |
| 6 | Staff welfare and connection | **The Musical** | A feel-good topic suits the most joyful format |
| 7 | Policy and governance | **The Courtroom** | Debates why a rule changed, with both sides heard |

**Start with the low-effort formats** (Infomercial, Nature Documentary, Breaking News) to build a rhythm. Move up to the Musical and the Heist once the team is comfortable.

## 4. Guardrails for Genre Episodes

1. **Parody the genre, not a specific work.** A heist movie, yes; a named film, no. Do not use any existing film's, show's, or song's title, characters, logo, set, catchphrases, or music. Parody rules differ by country, and I cannot give legal advice. Ask ADB's legal or communications team before borrowing anything from an existing work.
2. **Music must be original or licensed.** This workspace has AI audio tools connected (ElevenLabs and Higgsfield). I have not tested whether they can produce a usable song, or what their license terms allow. Check both before relying on them.
3. **The facts never go in costume.** The Recipe and the Receipt appear plainly in every format.
4. **No stereotypes.** Accents, nationalities, and cultures are never the joke, especially in the soap opera, documentary, and travel formats.
5. **Match the genre to the topic's weight.** Big comedy for small changes; calm formats (Picture-Book, Nature Documentary) for sensitive ones.
